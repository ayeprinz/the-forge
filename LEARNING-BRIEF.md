# The Forge learning brief

Teach from the necessary foundations with no assumed prior reading. Use an open pool of suitable books, primary research, historical documents, expert explanations and practical resources. A supplied reading list is a starting point, not a closed syllabus. Do not claim full-text access from a title, cover or excerpt. Explain the actual material consulted and its limits in source notes.

Organise learning into coherent sequences. Each lesson has a clear outcome, understandable prerequisites, substantive explanation, a worked example, five recall prompts with thinking time and a practical application. Revisit earlier ideas in new contexts. Track what has actually been published rather than assuming a planned topic has been learned. Listening does not establish mastery.

Reading books alongside the podcast is encouraged. Before revisiting a topic, try recalling an idea, check it against the source, correct the explanation, and return later. Do not describe rereading as automatic reconsolidation or promise a particular brain mechanism.

Keep the range broad: psychology and learning; communication and relationships; business and money; science and technology; history and institutions; philosophy, religion and spiritual traditions; creativity and practical skills. Match evidence to the kind of claim. Preserve differences between scientific evidence, historical interpretation, philosophical argument, spiritual teaching and metaphor.

Use two natural conversational voices. Revise both sides of each exchange together. Avoid invented listener biography, references to deleted anecdotes, unnecessary repetition, unsupported universal claims and recaps that merely repeat the previous hour. Use accurate bracketed topic labels. Length should serve the lesson rather than encourage padding.

Before publication, check dialogue continuity, source support, numerical examples, limitations, recall answers and the practical task. Archive replaced editions. Preserve an episode's identity when publishing a correction; change the audio filename so a new download can retrieve the revised recording.
